=== phd-meet-up-visite-de-lobservatoire-de-la-cote-dazur-site-de-calern
title: PhD meet-up – Visit of the Observatoire de la Côte d'Azur, Calern site
price: Free, registration required
price_fr: Gratuit, sur inscription
--- body
After the visit of the Mont Gros site, AJC06 invites you to visit the Calern site of the Observatoire de la Côte d'Azur!

- **Date**: Friday 19 July [new date]
- **Time**: 14:00 – 16:00
- **Place**: Observatoire de la Côte d'Azur, Calern site, 2130 route de l'Observatoire, 06460 Caussols

Limited places! The tour is in French.
**Registration is free but required.**

Discover an exceptional site near Grasse, whose natural and scientific heritage will amaze you.

On this guided tour, learn more about the scientific life of the plateau, past and present.
A little history: since the 1970s, the Calern plateau has hosted instruments that pioneered their fields, notably in laser ranging and interferometry. The Schmidt telescope – the largest in Europe in its class – was in intense use until the early 2000s for wide-field imaging of small objects. Since 1988, the Calern plateau has been an observing site of the Observatoire de la Côte d'Azur.

Come and see Antti Lovag's Bubble Houses and how they work as instrument shelters, walk across a moon-like plateau and discover the science being done today by the Observatoire's researchers and engineers. Some projects build on past experience, such as the special links between astronomy and geophysics or solar physics, while others are innovative projects involved in university training for and through research.

The Association of Young Researchers of the Alpes-Maritimes (AJC06) is delighted to invite all PhD candidates to an evening at the opera: *La Fille de Madame Angot*. The performance takes place on Saturday **28 September at 8 pm at the Nice Opera.**

**A worthy heir to Jacques Offenbach, the composer Charles Lecocq wrote in *La Fille de Madame Angot* a virtuoso score full of joy and mischief. Much more than light entertainment, it is an ode to hope and renewal.**

A huge box-office hit in the late 19th century, *La Fille de Madame Angot* is rarely performed today. Won over by the irresistible energy of the score, Richard Brunel brings Clairette Angot and her friends from Les Halles back on stage. To make the love story and political plot feel current again, the director of the Opéra de Lyon moved the action to Paris in May 1968, when artistic effervescence and political protest were at their height. Libertarian slogans and nods to the French New Wave breathe youth into the work, given fresh energy by conductor Chloé Dufresne.
